What the audit found

The scale is not missing. It is written down, with a comment calling 12px the absolute floor, and then bypassed almost everywhere. These are the sizes below the floor, counted by file.

File9px10px11px11.5pxMostly
Dashboard.jsx--5-Matrix cell captions, chart axis ticks
Inventory.jsx--6-Chart labels and axis ticks
Alerts.jsx--4-Severity labels, meta captions
SettingsMenu.jsx-113Section labels, the cost warning
Sidebar.jsx112-The alert count badge, "Switch"
StockPositionBar.jsx--2-Bar captions
Activity.jsx, Home.jsx, warehouse/--4-Uppercase section labels
Two of these are defensible and the rest are not. Chart axis ticks are conventionally small and sit beside a labelled axis, and the footer credit is exactly the kind of low-value-but-present text you described. The uppercase section labels are the real problem: uppercase at 11px is harder to read than sentence case at 11px, because the shape of a word is part of how it is recognised, and uppercase throws that shape away.

What the standards actually say

There is no single answer, but there is a clear split, and knowing which side of it StockSense sits on decides the scale.

SourceBodyFloorIts position
WCAG 2.2--Sets no size, but requires text to survive 200% zoom without loss. Small fixed sizes usually fail this before large ones do.
Apple HIG, macOS13px11pxDense desktop chrome, on the assumption of a close-viewed high density display.
Apple HIG, iOS17px11pxNotably larger body than macOS. Apple does not have one answer either.
Material 314px11pxBody medium at 14, and it treats 11 as a label size, never running text.
Tailwind / web default16px12px16px is the browser default body size and the de facto web floor.
Linear, Stripe13-14px11pxDeliberately dense. They are tools used all day by people who chose them.
StockSense today17px13pxLarge print Past every standard here, deliberately. The declared floor is real in CSS; 30 inline styles still undercut it.
Where this landed. StockSense is a desktop tool, which argues for density, but it is demoed on a projector and built for warehouse staff who skew older, which argues harder the other way. It now sits past the Tailwind end of the table: a 17px body and a real 13px floor. The other half of the fix was spending the range on fewer steps rather than smaller ones. Seven steps over this range crowded the bottom, so --text-md, which sat one pixel from the body size and named a job font-weight already does, is gone.
