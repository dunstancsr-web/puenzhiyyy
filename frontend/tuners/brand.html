四海米行Four Seas Rice Trading

A name for the fictional SME this tool is built for, derived from Puenzhi, and a calligraphic mark to go with it. Everything here is one self-contained page: the brush face is embedded, so nothing depends on a font server staying up.

Where the name comes from

Puenzhi breaks into two Dzongkha parts: pün (སྤུན), sibling or kin, and zhi (བཞི), four. So it is closer to the four siblings than to four friends, and that distinction drove the Chinese choice. 朋友 (péngyǒu) is the ordinary word for friend and is too weak: the register wanted is kinship, the bond you do not choose.

CandidateReadingWhat it carries
四海 sì hǎi
four seas		 Recommendedthe pick
From the Analects (顏淵): 四海之内皆兄弟也 — within the four seas, all men are brothers. Two characters that carry both halves of Puenzhi at once: the number four, and kinship rather than acquaintance. It is also exactly the register an overseas-Chinese trading house uses, and it quietly says importer, which this business is: the SKUs come from Thailand, Vietnam, India and Japan.
四友 sì yǒu
four friends		 The literal translation, with a literary echo in 文房四友, the four friends of the scholar's desk. Handsome, but it reads boutique rather than commercial, and friends is the weaker reading of Puenzhi.
四合 sì hé
four joined		 Joined or in harmony, echoing 四合院, the courtyard house built around a family. Warm and domestic, but rare as a firm name, so it would read as invented.
The suffix matters as much as the name. 米行 (mǐháng) is the traditional term for a rice dealer, where 行 means a trading house, as in 銀行. It is what a family rice business in Singapore or Malaysia actually calls itself. 米業 is the modern corporate alternative and sounds like a larger group; 貿易 is generic. For an SME, 米行 is the credible one.

How to explain it, out loud

Written to be said, not read. Lead with the team name: a judge cannot hold an unfamiliar phrase and its explanation at the same time, so give them the familiar thing first and let the Chinese be the payoff.

Fifteen seconds — the one to use

Our team name is Puenzhiyyy — Bhutanese for four siblings, because there are four of us.

So we named our client to match: 四海米行, Four Seas Rice Trading. 四海 means four seas, and it is from a line in Confucius: within the four seas, all men are brothers.

So the name keeps both halves of ours: the four, and the family. And it happens to describe the business, because their rice comes in from four different countries — Thailand, Vietnam, India, Japan.

The red stamp is 米. It is the character for rice.

If you only get one sentence

Our team name means four siblings, so we named the client Four Seas Rice Trading, from the Chinese saying within the four seas, all men are brothers. Four, and family, same as us.

The line that does the work is "it keeps both halves of ours: the four, and the family." That is the beat where a judge realises the name was derived rather than decorated. Land it and the rest is colour. The Thailand / Vietnam / India / Japan detail is worth keeping because it is checkable on screen in the Inventory tab, which turns a nice story into evidence that the data was thought about.
"Did you just make that up?"
No. 四海之内皆兄弟 is from the Analects. And 米行 is not invented either: it is the traditional word for a rice dealer, what family rice shops in Singapore and Malaysia actually call themselves. 行 means trading house, the same character as in 銀行, bank.
"Why only one character in the stamp?"
米 is rice, and it is the only part that survives being shrunk to a favicon. Four characters at that size is a red smudge.
"What exactly does Puenzhi mean?"
Do not over-claim the Dzongkha. "pun is sibling or kin, zhi is four" is as far as it is worth going: an accurate decomposition, offered as what the team name means to us rather than a linguistic claim to defend.

A is the better product decision and the worse demo decision: the judges are evaluating StockSense, and burying it in the footer to win realism trades the wrong thing. C is honest but cramped, and co-branding implies a partnership that does not exist. B keeps StockSense named, makes the SME concrete, and the switchable-looking chip does useful extra work: it implies the product has other customers, which is the claim a hackathon pitch wants to make anyway.

Notes

Why cinnabar and not a brand red. #b3271e is the colour of 印泥, the cinnabar paste a physical seal is pressed into. A brighter red would read as a generic logo; this one reads as an object that exists.
The typeface is Ma Shan Zheng, a brush script under the SIL Open Font License, subset here to nine glyphs, 3.7 KB from 5.86 MB. Free to use commercially. A true seal-script (篆書) face would be more authentic for the square mark, but nothing usable is openly licensed, and a wrong licence on a demo logo is a worse problem than a slightly modern brush.
