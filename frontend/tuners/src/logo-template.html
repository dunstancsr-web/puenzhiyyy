四海米行Four Seas Rice Trading

A name for the fictional SME this tool is built for, derived from Puenzhi, and a calligraphic mark to go with it. Everything here is one self-contained page: the brush face is embedded, so nothing depends on a font server staying up.

Where the name comes from

Puenzhi breaks into two Dzongkha parts: pün (སྤུན), sibling or kin, and zhi (བཞི), four. So it is closer to the four siblings than to four friends, and that distinction drove the Chinese choice. 朋友 (péngyǒu) is the ordinary word for friend and is too weak: the register wanted is kinship, the bond you do not choose.

四海 sì hǎi
four seas		 Recommendedthe pick
From the Analects (顏淵): 四海之内皆兄弟也 — within the four seas, all men are brothers. Two characters that carry both halves of Puenzhi at once: the number four, and kinship rather than acquaintance. It is also exactly the register an overseas-Chinese trading house uses, and it quietly says importer, which this business is: the SKUs come from Thailand, Vietnam, India and Japan.
四友 sì yǒu
four friends		 The literal translation, with a literary echo in 文房四友, the four friends of the scholar's desk. Handsome, but it reads boutique rather than commercial, and friends is the weaker reading of Puenzhi.
四合 sì hé
four joined		 Joined or in harmony, echoing 四合院, the courtyard house built around a family. Warm and domestic, but rare as a firm name, so it would read as invented.
The suffix matters as much as the name. 米行 (mǐháng) is the traditional term for a rice dealer, where 行 means a trading house, as in 銀行. It is what a family rice business in Singapore or Malaysia actually calls itself. 米業 is the modern corporate alternative and sounds like a larger group; 貿易 is generic. For an SME, 米行 is the credible one.

Notes

Why cinnabar and not a brand red. #b3271e is the colour of 印泥, the cinnabar paste a physical seal is pressed into. A brighter red would read as a generic logo; this one reads as an object that exists.
The typeface is Ma Shan Zheng, a brush script under the SIL Open Font License, subset here to nine glyphs, 3.7 KB from 5.86 MB. Free to use commercially. A true seal-script (篆書) face would be more authentic for the square mark, but nothing usable is openly licensed, and a wrong licence on a demo logo is a worse problem than a slightly modern brush.
